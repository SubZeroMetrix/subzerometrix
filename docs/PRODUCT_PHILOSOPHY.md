## Why Metrix Exists

Service businesses lose revenue quietly — a follow-up that never happens, an estimate that goes cold, a customer nobody checked on. Most software either buries that signal in noise or hands total control to an AI with no human oversight. Metrix Command Center exists to do neither: surface what actually needs attention, and let a governed AI team recommend next actions that a human always approves before anything happens.

## Problems We Solve

- Missed follow-ups, stalled estimates, and at-risk customers that go unnoticed until it's too late.
- Customer and job data scattered across disconnected tools instead of one governed system of record.
- The false choice between "no AI help" and "AI that acts on its own without oversight."

## Who We Serve

Service and contractor businesses — the same population the founder spent 24+ years working inside as a technician, service manager, and facilities leader. Product decisions are made from lived operational experience, not outsider assumption.

## Product Principles

- **Evidence over hype.** Every feature described in marketing must be real and shipped. Internally, every AI recommendation must be grounded in real account data — never a fabricated or generic suggestion.
- **Outcome-driven, not feature-driven.** The measure of a feature is whether it helps a real business recover revenue or avoid a dropped customer — not how impressive it looks in a demo.
- **Simplicity.** Prefer a smaller set of things that genuinely work over a large set of shell features. See `ENGINEERING_CONSTITUTION.md`'s "no shell builds" rule — it is a product principle as much as an engineering one.
- **Customer Zero philosophy.** The founder's own operational background (facilities management, technician supervision, service management) functions as an internal "customer zero" check: does this feature match how a real service business actually runs, or how software companies imagine one runs?

## UX Philosophy

Clear, confident, uncluttered. A first-time visitor or new user should understand what the product does and why it's trustworthy within seconds — not after decoding jargon. Visual weight should be proportional to importance: the product's real differentiator (human-approved AI) gets real visual and narrative space, not a single line buried in a feature grid.

## Continuous Improvement

Product direction is revised based on verified evidence — real usage, real feedback, real gaps found in audits — not speculation. Recent example: a full landing-page audit (visual, conversion, SEO, accessibility) was used to drive a concrete, evidence-based improvement pass rather than a generic redesign.

## Customer Zero Philosophy

Before any feature ships, it should be evaluated against the question: "would this have actually helped me when I was running facilities/service operations day to day?" This is not a formal customer research process (none is claimed to exist yet) — it is the founder's direct operational lens, applied consistently, and should remain a first-line filter even as the team grows.
